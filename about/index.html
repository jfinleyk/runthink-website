About RunThink

You're already moving.
You may as well think.

RunThink turns the time you already spend moving into structured thinking time. One question a day, arriving before you head out, building toward something over the course of a month.

Most of us do our best thinking in motion. The problem is that the thinking is unguided. It's the same three worries on a loop, mile after mile. RunThink gives that time a direction. You don't have to journal for an hour or sit still with your eyes closed. You just have to go, and take one question with you.

How the system works

Three layers, nested. The month sets the direction, the week narrows it, the day gives you something you can actually carry on a run.

Month

A theme

Every month opens on a single theme: self-talk, fear, comparison, patience. Big enough to be worth a month, specific enough to notice in your own life. It's the through-line for everything that follows.

Week

A focus

The theme breaks into four weekly focuses, each one an angle on it. If the month is self-talk, one week might look at the voice you use when you fail, another at what you say to yourself in the last mile.

Day

A question

Each morning you get one question inside that week's focus. Think it through while you move. When you're done, write what came up in your private journal, or share it with the forum and read what everyone else found.

The layering is the point. A single good question is a nice moment; thirty of them pointed at the same thing is how something actually shifts. Miss a day and nothing breaks. The theme is still there tomorrow.

Built on CBT

The questions aren't generated at random. They're built on cognitive behavioral therapy, the most studied framework in modern psychology and the one most of us have never been walked through.

Thoughts drive feelings. Feelings drive actions. Change the thought and the rest follows.

The core of CBT, in one line.

CBT works by noticing a thought, examining whether it's actually true, and choosing a more accurate one. That's it. And it's exactly the sort of work a long run is good for. You have the time. You have no screen. You're already in your own head.

Notice Catch the thought you didn't know you were having.
Examine Ask what the evidence for it actually is.
Reframe Replace it with something truer and more useful.

Every daily question moves you through some part of that loop. Over a month, the loop starts running on its own, which is the whole goal. You shouldn't need the app to think clearly. You should need it until you don't.

RunThink is CBT-informed self-reflection, not therapy. It isn't a substitute for care from a licensed professional, and it isn't crisis support. If you're struggling, please reach out to someone who can help.

Meant for everyone

There is no pace requirement, no minimum distance, no leaderboard. It's for runners, walkers, rollers, cyclists, swimmers, golfers, and anyone else who moves and thinks at the same time. A twenty-minute walk around the block counts exactly as much as a twenty-mile long run, because the question is the same and so is the thinking.

You don't need to be an athlete, and you don't need to have anything wrong with you. The questions are written for a person, not a demographic. No jargon, no assumed background in psychology, nothing that requires you to already be someone who journals.

A community answering the same question

Because everyone gets the same question on the same day, the forum doesn't work like a feed. It reads like one conversation: what you found on your run this morning, next to what a walker in another city found on the same question. Someone else's answer will regularly unlock the thing you couldn't quite get to.

Sharing is always optional. Every question can go to your private journal instead, and plenty of people never post at all. But most mindfulness practice is solitary by default, and the community is what keeps this one from being. Thinking alongside other people, it turns out, is its own kind of practice.

Common questions

What is RunThink?

RunThink is a mindfulness app for people in motion. Each morning it delivers one question, built on cognitive behavioral therapy, to think through while you run, walk, roll, or ride. Afterward you can journal what came up privately or share it with a community answering the same question.

Is RunThink free?

Yes. RunThink is free on iPhone. An optional Premium subscription is $4.99 per year.

Does RunThink have a community?

Yes. Everyone receives the same question each day, so the forum reads like one shared conversation rather than a feed. Sharing is optional. Every question can go to a private journal instead.

Do I have to be a runner?

No. RunThink is for runners, walkers, rollers, cyclists, swimmers, and anyone else who moves and thinks at the same time. There's no pace requirement, no minimum distance, and no leaderboard.

How is RunThink different from meditation apps?

Meditation apps ask you to sit still. RunThink uses the movement time you already have, and instead of clearing your mind it gives your mind one structured thing to work on: a monthly theme, narrowed weekly, delivered as one daily question.

Is RunThink therapy?

No. RunThink is CBT-informed self-reflection, not therapy, and it isn't a substitute for care from a licensed professional or for crisis support.
